Each principal gets: timeline, the wound, psychology, voice, appearance, relationships, what the Agency file says (and gets wrong), series arc, and canon anchors already established in the Draft One text that must survive any rewrite.*

## WREN HALE — Captain, 34

### Timeline
- **1992** — born in Ketchikan, Alaska. Father: **Gus Hale**, salmon-boat owner-operator (F/V *Marta June*, named for his mother). Mother: **Diane Hale**, elementary-school teacher.
- **1992–2009** — a childhood measured in seasons: school years with Diane, summers on the boat with Gus. Learns weather, engines, knots, silence, and the price of pride from the deck of a fishing boat. Watches float planes cross the sound and understands them as *the way out that also stays connected*.
- **2009 (age 17)** — the envelope. A bad season, and Gus still puts a summer's cash on the table: *"One of us should get to look down at the water for a change."* Adds a used aluminum E6-B bought off a retiring bush pilot, paint worn off the wind side. She solos before her eighteenth birthday.
- **2010–2013** — ratings at a windswept part-61 school in Ketchikan and later Anchorage; instructs briefly; flies right seat on floatplane tours. Develops the habit that defines her: written personal minimums, reviewed monthly, initialed.
- **2014 (age 22)** — hired by an Anchorage freight outfit. Piper Chieftains. Ice, darkness, fish smell that never leaves the charts. Becomes "current isn't proficient" — first as her own private rule, then out loud.
- **2016 (age 25)** — Gus dies. Engine-room accident, nothing dramatic; boats are honest about their risks. He never flew with her — weather, seasons, always a better weekend coming. She flies home for the funeral through the pass he used to watch her cross. The shoebox of expired charts begins here (his death makes the archive sacred).
- **2017–2018** — the Zulu winter (Draft One canon): a senior captain flies known SLD icing for the schedule and is celebrated for it; the next pilot into the same system — Wren — turns around at the first side-window splash and is told she "cost the company a client." Writes her first formal minimums card that night. Leaves Alaska within the year — Diane stays, in the house above the Ketchikan harbor, still teaching third grade; it's Wren who can't live in a town made of Gus-shaped absences. The distance between mother and daughter is emotional, not geographic: Diane never left, and some part of Wren has never forgiven herself for the fact that she did.
- **2018 (age 26)** — hired at **Cascade Air Group** (Boeing Field; 135 charter, Navajos → King Airs). Meets Captain **Danny Ruiz** (dossier below). Within a year they are flying together; within two they are together. Rule one and rule two do not exist yet — Ruiz is where they come from.
- **2021 (age 29)** — the fall. Wren discovers Ruiz has been pencil-whipping training records and duty-time logs across the operation — including some with her name on them. She reports it internally, then to the FSDO when internal dies quietly. Cascade needs its golden captain; the story gets rewritten: *difficult, emotional, a problem hire with a grudge*. Colleagues who know better — Jax Calloway among them — say nothing. She is "let go for cause"; Ruiz gets a tail-number transfer and a promotion arc. Two years of consulting-gig wilderness follow. The industry's version of her is the only version in circulation.
- **2023 (age 31)** — **Dale Voss**, administering a 135 check for a tiny operator she's contracting for, watches her fly, reads the Cascade file *sideways*, and makes one phone call. **Marcus Griffin** hires her without asking a single question about Cascade. She has been Meridian's captain — and its de facto standards department — ever since.
- **2024–2026** — the Elias arrangement: **Elias Ward, 38**, a marine structural engineer in Ballard she met over a dock inspection gone long. They see each other when the season allows — dinner, a night, no questions, no futures — which is precisely the design: intimacy scheduled, contained, and unable to cost her anything. It is the most honest dishonest thing in her life, and it dissolves mid-Book 1 when the couple cover makes even contained things impossible. Her ending text is four words; his reply — "Figured. Good luck, Captain." — is its entire eulogy.
- **2026 (age 34)** — Book 1 opens. Three years of rebuilt reputation, guarded by design, running the safest small flight department in the Pacific Northwest.

### The wound
Two-layered. Layer one is Ruiz: *love let someone use my integrity as a resource, and the institution sided with the liar.* Layer two is older and quieter: *the people I love die or drift with the important things unsaid* — Gus never got the flight; Diane is still in Ketchikan, in the same house, and she and Wren orbit each other at holiday-card distance across two thousand miles Wren chose. (Series use: Book 1's Alaska legs put Wren *over* home without landing — the Aleutian diversion is the closest she's been in years; Book 3's thaw is a landing.) Both layers produce the same behavior: plan alternates for everything, keep the archive sealed, never need anyone enough that their leaving changes your heading.

### Psychology
- **Wants:** a flawless operation; a life with no exposure; to never again be in a story someone else gets to tell.
- **Needs:** to let one person hold something she can't control — and to learn that discretion and armor are not the same thing (the explicit series thesis for her).
- **Defenses:** procedure as emotion-containment; deadpan threat-comedy; generosity delivered sideways (she'll fight for Priya's job, Manny's kid's braces, Griff's dignity — anything but ask for something herself).
- **Contradictions that make her real:** ruthlessly unsentimental in speech, secretly the most sentimental person in the series (the shoebox, the E6-B, the pocket archive). Physically fearless, emotionally risk-averse. Hates lying — is about to be *great* at cover work, which horrifies her (Book 1's manifest problem is aimed directly at this fault line).
- **The manifest problem (Book 1 core):** the mission requires her to sign falsified paperwork — Ruiz's sin, with her name on it. Her resolution: she does it with open eyes, having interrogated Okafor about the difference until she believes it (*Ruiz lied to protect himself; this protects someone else, and I sign my own name*). But it costs her sleep, and she never lets the crew see the bill. ⚠ In Book 3, that signature is exactly what makes her mole-plausible on paper — the file reads "has falsified official documents under pressure."

### Voice & mannerisms
Flat declaratives; threats delivered like fuel loads ("you're one more remark from losing your water"); "adequate" as maximum praise; questions as tests. Swears rarely — a curse from Wren means the situation is genuinely bad. Physical tells: squares paperwork when buying time; touches the zippered chest pocket when moved; eats peanut M&Ms in ritual fours; drinks coffee deliberately lukewarm; hums Patsy Cline doing weight-and-balance and denies it. Says the biggest things from doorways, half over her shoulder — until character growth lets her say one dead-ahead.

### Appearance
5'7", built like someone who has thrown cargo in the dark: capable, economical. Dark hair kept in a working knot; gray eyes (Jack, ch. 7: "like a stable marine layer"). Wardrobe: flight-department utilitarian plus one good dress per crisis. Wears her father's watch, band replaced twice.

### What the Agency file says ⚠
Voss's original assessment memo, quoted in Book 2: *"HALE: the rarest profile — integrity with a paper trail. Reported her own captain, took the industry hit, never once shopped the story to save herself. Sat out two years rather than sign someone else's version. If she agrees to carry something, it arrives. Weakness: she will burn herself for the crew without telling anyone she's on fire. Handle honestly or not at all."* The file gets one thing wrong: it lists her as "no significant attachments." The file was written before Jack.

### Series arc
- **B1:** the manifest decision; learns her integrity can serve something bigger than her record; ends Elias; shares more hotel rooms than she doesn't; and, the night after the asset is safe, stops pretending the wall between the beds ever worked — then refuses, with catastrophic professionalism, to discuss it.
- **B2:** learns to carry a cover without becoming it; teaches Sorokina's daughter to fly straight-and-level (echo of Gus); the undiscussed arrangement keeps happening until the silence becomes the most expensive thing she owns — the words, at last, end of book.
- **B3:** the suspicion inverts Cascade — this time an institution she *chose* doubts her; the growth is that she doesn't armor up alone: she lets the crew fight for her. Ends: engaged, archive open, and one flight north to Ketchikan — Diane's kitchen, the harbor, the things finally said.

### Canon anchors (survive all rewrites)
Ketchikan; the envelope + line; the E6-B; the shoebox; "current isn't proficient"; personal minimums card initialed monthly; M&Ms double freezer-bagged as "ballast"; sleeps nearest the door; rules one and two; Cascade/Ruiz; hired via Voss's call and Griff's no-questions.

## JACK MARSH — First Officer, 31

### Timeline
- **1995** — born in Des Moines, Washington. Father: **Sam Marsh**, Boeing 727 flight engineer, later a Part 91 corporate operation's dispatcher-of-all-trades. Mother: **Ellen Marsh**, wildlife photographer (birds), part-time school photographer for money.
- **1995–2013** — grows up bilingual in Aviation and Ellen. Sam's world: checklists, systems, the Truck (the V35B Bonanza, bought 1998, hail dimples and all). Ellen's world: patience, watching, the two-knock doorframe ritual every time anyone left the house. Jack is the kid who makes neighbor kids do bike walkarounds (the quarry-ride photo lives in an album).
- **2013–2017** — ratings through a busy Puget Sound flight school while working its front desk; instructs part-time before he can rent a car. Discovers the thing he's best at: making complicated things feel learnable — and making people feel seen while they learn.
- **2017–2022 (ages 22–27)** — freight: a Navajo for a scrappy outfit running Puget Sound and the coast. Five years of real IMC, real ice, real single-pilot discipline. Applies to the regionals late (Ellen's diagnosis interrupts).
- **2020 (age 25)** — Ellen dies in spring: eighteen months of illness she narrated with a photographer's calm. The doorframe knock migrates to the glareshield. Sam goes quiet for a year; Jack moves back to the peninsula side of the water, keeps flying freight, holds the family together with cheerfulness deployed like a rescue line.
- **2022–2025 (ages 27–30)** — leaves freight for full-time instructing (a CFII with a waiting list; Bergman the ER doctor is his; the "you built this" text is canon). On a regional's hiring list from 2023.
- **Early 2025 (age 29)** — the class date lands — and days later Sam drops in the Safeway produce aisle. Left MCA stroke. Jack declines the class from a hospital corridor ("some decisions don't have a brief") and never re-litigates it. Instructs on; runs Sam's recovery like a training syllabus.
- **Dating history** — Jack is bisexual, and has been out to his family since he was nineteen (Sam's complete reaction, canon-to-be: "Okay. Does he like airplanes?"). A college boyfriend who taught him about jazz; a freight-years girlfriend who taught him about endings; and since 2025, **Aaron Brandt, 33**, a Harborview ER charge nurse — an easy, kind, no-questions arrangement built on incompatible schedules and mutual emotional unavailability. Aaron ends it gently mid-Book 1 with the observation Jack isn't ready to hear: "I'm not competing with your fake girlfriend. I've seen how you look at her, man." (Series note: his bisexuality is texture, not plotline — with one reserved thematic beat where BRIDGEBELL drafts his cover bio "simpler" and Jack makes them correct it: the best covers are true.)
- **Fall 2026 (age 31)** — freshly through a 135 refresher and his 135.293/297 checks, hired at Meridian on a Tuesday. Book 1 opens on his first morning of the Okafor account.

### The wound
Ellen's death taught him that the worst forecasts verify — so he treats every day like VFR-on-top: enjoy the sun, know the tops, never trust it to last. His charm is real *and* it is weather-proofing: if everyone's laughing, nobody's leaving, nobody's dying, nobody's asking how Jack is. Second layer: the deferred-dream ledger — he genuinely doesn't regret choosing Sam, but he carries a quiet fear that his life is a series of graceful accommodations rather than choices, which is why Book 1's mission (and Wren) hit so hard: they're the first things he's *chosen* at full throttle since the hospital corridor.

### Psychology
- **Wants:** everyone he loves safe, fed, laughing, and within twenty minutes' drive.
- **Needs:** to be taken seriously — by himself. To discover that his gifts (reading people, building legends, making anyone believe anything) are not "the funny guy's tricks" but formidable, and to own what that implies. ⚠ The tradecraft arc is precisely this: Book 2 forces him to face that he *enjoys* deception, and to decide what kind of man wields that safely. His answer — Ellen's answer — is: one who never lies at home.
- **Defenses:** performance; teacherly deflection (answering feelings-questions with lessons); rating everything (FBOs, coffee, days) to keep judgment external and light.
- **Contradictions:** an open book about facts, a vault about grief (the tap stayed unexplained for two months — canon); patient as weather with everyone but himself; the most honest liar in the series.

### Voice & mannerisms
Warm, quick, structured — even his jokes have a setup-payoff discipline (instructor's cadence, canon). Deploys "sweetheart" tactically; names adversaries (Gary the jammer, canon); two knocks on the glareshield before every takeoff (canon: Ellen); the one-shoulder "small shrug" for true things; goes *quiet and precise* when genuinely frightened — the tell Wren learns to read. Never runs in rain.

### Appearance
6'1", lean, dark blond, habitual half-smile; dresses like a man whose closet is 40% flight-department polo. Drives the blue Subaru with the mismatched fender (canon — the fender is from teaching Bergman's kid to drive; he'll never fix it).

### What the Agency file says ⚠
Okafor's own assessment, Book 1: *"MARSH: natural elicitor, improvisational legend-builder, zero training. Subjects volunteer information to him and thank him afterward. Motivation profile unusual: not money, not thrill, not flag — attachment. He will do dangerous things excellently for the people inside his fence. Recommend: put the mission inside the fence."* What the file gets wrong: it flags his humor as a security risk. It is the opposite — it's his cover, and it never once slips where it matters.

### Series arc
- **B1:** the audition he doesn't know he's passing → the mission he chooses with open eyes; discovers the work fits him disturbingly well; loses Aaron to a truth he won't say out loud; and ends the book in the last hotel room of the operation having finally stopped performing — followed by a morning-after in which two fluent communicators say nothing at all.
- **B2:** formal tradecraft; the "I'm good at lying" reckoning; Sam tension (first time in his life he can't tell his father the truth); discovers he minds — enormously — that the arrangement has no name; wins the words by never once pushing — end of book.
- **B3:** the overheard debrief detonates his exact wound (was *I* an accommodation? was *she* tasked?); Clause Four; the crucible; learns to ask the question instead of armoring in charm. Ends: on one knee in Friday Harbor with an unread speech.

### Canon anchors
The glareshield double-tap and Ellen's doorframe origin; "some decisions don't have a brief"; the remarks column ("current isn't proficient — fix: hand-fly..."); Bergman; the archived airline email; walks unhurried through rain; the coaster ("I'M NOT GOING ANYWHERE"); teaches through mnemonics; sixty-six-HIT.

## ADAEZE OKAFOR — 71. Hotelier. Other things.

### Timeline
- **1955** — born in Lagos, third daughter of a customs clerk and a market trader; grows up multilingual (Igbo, Yoruba, English, market-stall French) and observant — the family business is reading people at a counter.
- **1977** — degree in accountancy, University of Lagos; hired by an international hotel group as a junior auditor. Discovers she can walk a property once and know which manager is stealing.
- **1978 (age 23)** — the harmattan airport, the calm man on the suitcase: **Bernard Okafor** — Nigerian-American, officially a hotel-development consultant, actually a CIA officer under non-official cover working West African commercial networks. ⚠ *The meeting was a bump*: Adaeze had been spotted as access-agent material (her audits touched half the money in Lagos). What Bernard's file never predicted: he fell for the recruit.
- **1978–1981** — she works with him — first unwitting-adjacent, then witting; is very, very good; refuses formal recruitment twice on principle ("I do not work for people who lie to me").
- **1981 (Nairobi)** — the grounded flight. Before the proposal, Bernard commits the sin of his career: he shows her the file — the bump, the assessment memo, all of it — and *then* says the line about the arithmetic. She marries him eleven days later, eyes fully open. This is the root of her lifelong method: **the truth is the strongest cover** — and of her rule that she never lies to the people she recruits about anything except what she must.
- **1981–2015** — the double life as one life: forty hotels across three continents, each one real, profitable, and quietly useful — safehouses, meeting floors, staff who notice things. Adaeze naturalizes American in 1988; formally becomes an officer (NOC) in 1990. Two open secrets in the marriage: no children (a grief she reassigned into goddaughters, scholarships, and staff who become family — Chidinma's mother was her first hire in London), and the standing agreement that whoever survived the other would keep the network alive.
- **2015** — Bernard's diagnosis; they retire "fully" and spend seven years being only married. She calls these the profitable years.
- **2022** — Bernard dies (41 years — canon). Within six months she is bored, furious at being bored, and back on contract: **BRIDGEBELL**, her design — deniable civilian logistics on the Pacific Rim, built from businesses that are real. First acquisition: a Gulfstream in a trust. First problem: a crew she can trust with it.
- **2025–2026** — finds Meridian (via Voss); watches; the fake-couple accident hands her the final piece. Book 1: the Sorokina crisis forces her hand early.

### The wound
Bernard's death removed the only person on earth cleared for all of her. Her charm, her matchmaking, her "entertainments" are not manipulation — they are a widow's raging appetite for *true things happening in front of her*. The series' quiet tragedy: she can give Wren and Jack the life she had, or the work she had, and she knows better than anyone that carrying both nearly requires a miracle. She recruits them anyway. ⚠ Book 3 makes her answer for that.

### Psychology
- **Wants:** the network strong, the asset safe, the crew happy — in that order professionally and the reverse order at two a.m.
- **Needs:** to forgive herself for enjoying the game more than peace; to let someone (Chidinma) inherit her instead of just her methods.
- **Method:** truth-forward tradecraft; tests disguised as generosity; power worn as warmth. Never raises her voice — the executive appears only in stillness (canon: "I made no decisions before tea. After tea I made several").

### Voice & mannerisms
Grand, unhurried, theatrical; laying words down "like cards" (canon gesture); ginger tea, no ice, at top of climb (canon); addresses people as "children," "my aviators," "dears"; proverbs that turn out to be doctrine ("everything that matters starts with weather" — which was Bernard's line first).

### Series arc
- **B1:** the unmasking as necessity; carries the moral weight of asking Wren for the manifest.
- **B2:** mentor and program chief; the reveal of Bernard's file trick becomes her recruiting sermon.
- **B3:** her program is the one under suspicion; she must choose between protecting BRIDGEBELL and protecting the crew, and — because she is who she is — finds the line where both are true, at real cost. Final scene: at the Friday Harbor breakfast, one table over, having arranged nothing for once.

### Canon anchors
Harmattan story; Nairobi proposal; 41 years; forty hotels; ginger tea; Chidinma; "I am never angry at the truth before nine a.m."; the gala instincts; "nobody quotes my aviators out of context twice."

## DR. YELENA SOROKINA — 44. The asset. *(New, v2)*

### Timeline
- **1982** — born in Zhukovsky, outside Moscow — an aviation-city childhood; her father tested navigation systems, her mother taught mathematics. English learned from smuggled aviation manuals and Jeppesen charts (canon hook: textbook-perfect phraseology, charmingly formal).
- **2004–2012** — radiophysics degrees; recruited into a GRU-adjacent research institute working satellite-navigation vulnerabilities. Believes, sincerely, that she is building *shields* — protecting her own country's aircraft from exactly the spoofing she studies.
- **2010** — marries a flight-test engineer; **Mara** born 2011. Widowed 2019 (test-flight accident, the institute's own program; the state gave her a medal and a pension and never an honest report — the first crack).
- **2020–2024** — her portfolio is folded into an offensive targeting directorate under **Col. Ruslan Verin** (below). She watches her shields become spears — including applications against civil aviation that she cannot unknow. Begins, in 2024, passing carefully chosen material through a contact cultivated in the restaurant of an Okafor hotel in Helsinki (BRIDGEBELL's collection at work).
- **2026** — a counterintelligence sweep closes in; she runs from a Singapore conference with Mara and two suitcases; goes to ground in staff quarters of the Okafor hotel in Sapporo. Book 1 begins nine days later.

### Psychology & voice
Brilliant, exact, allergic to handling ("Do not manage me. Brief me."); grief-dry humor; treats the flight deck as the one place on earth where the rules are honest. Her defection is not ideology but *refusal of complicity* — which makes her Wren's mirror (both women ended their careers over falsified purposes) and the natural second teacher of the series: from the jump seat, the woman who builds jammers explains why the crew's green-needle discipline is the only thing her old directorate genuinely fears.

### Series function
- **B1:** the package who becomes a person; her competence turns the exfil into a three-pilot problem-solving story rather than a rescue of a victim.
- **B2:** consulting voice from the safehouse; the GPS-interference exercises are hers; her intelligence starts the Pacific Corporate thread.
- **B3:** ⚠ Verin's pursuit of her is the engine; her warning ("he will not jam your radios; he will jam your trust") names the book.

## MARA SOROKINA — 15. *(New, v2 — recommend keep)*

Sharp-tongued, terrified, pretending to be neither; learned English from cartoons and her mother's manuals, so she sounds like a sitcom teenager quoting a POH. Book 1: her passport problem is the pickup's complication; she bonds with Jack over naming things (he lets her name the Gulfstream's autopilot) and with Wren over silence — Wren teaches her straight-and-level in cruise, hands on the yoke, the way Gus never got to. Books 2–3: American high school as deep cover comedy; one Book 3 beat where the crew's protection of her is what breaks the case open. Function: raises stakes, humanizes the mission, gives Wren a relationship that has nothing to do with Jack — and gives the series its future-generation echo (the epilogue teases her first solo).

## COL. RUSLAN VERIN — 52. The adversary. *(New, v2)*

GRU signals/navigation-warfare officer; Sorokina's former patron — he pulled her into the directorate, praised her, promoted her, and genuinely believed she shared his cold arithmetic (deterrence requires spears). Her defection is therefore personal twice over: professional catastrophe and something close to apostasy. Methodical, unshowy, contemptuous of thugs; his weapon is process — manifests, cameras, patterns, patience. Book 1: a search grid and a voice on other people's phones; one near-miss at a Sapporo FBO he never knows was a near-miss. Book 2: identifies the *airplane* from fuel-uplift records. Book 3: on-stage antagonist; author of the sabotage; undone not by force but by the one variable his process can't model — a crew that tells each other the truth. Guardrail: never cartoonish; he is what Sorokina would have become if she'd stopped asking questions.

## THE HOME CAST

### Sam Marsh — 68. Retired 727 flight engineer.
Timeline: Boeing panel 1981–2004 (the 727's retirement broke his heart before the airline could); then twenty years as a corporate flight department's dispatcher/mother-hen. Married Ellen 1985 — she flew home from the 1998 Bonanza pre-buy photographing clouds and named the airplane the Truck (canon). Widowed 2020; stroke early 2025; BasicMed comeback 2026 (canon arc). The series' hearth: bad coffee, porch light, "fly good today." ⚠ Book 3 beat: the old flight engineer recognizes agency handwriting a generation early — he flew "special" charters out of Clark in the seventies and knows exactly what a too-clean trust looks like; he says nothing until saying something saves everyone. Never fully witting until the epilogue; his dignity about it is the point.

### Chidinma Adeyemi — 26. Barrister. Vetting officer.
Okafor's goddaughter — her mother ran Adaeze's first London property; the "family firm" raised her on discretion. Commercial-fraud barrister (real, excellent) whose chambers work doubles as BRIDGEBELL's legal/vetting arm — contractor, not Agency staff, which lets her say *"I don't work for anyone's government; I work for my aunt, who is worse."* Book 1: builds the cover passengers and the paperwork; her Draft One "diligence" scenes survive almost verbatim, now literal. Book 2: handler-of-record and Wren's first real girlfriend-who-knows-everything. Book 3: the one who refuses to let the mole file swallow Wren. Voice: cut-glass precision, verdicts as kindness.

### Marcus "Griff" Griffin — 58. Owner. Never witting.
Twenty-two years of freight; married Ellen-adjacent in spirit — his own late wife **Ellen Griffin's** insurance money is in Meridian (canon; two Ellens is intentional texture: both households built on a beloved ghost). Bought the company because "the only version of being alive I've ever understood has a ramp attached" (canon). Series function: the innocent the whole cast protects; comic relief whose panic invented the couple and therefore, unknowingly, the cover legend of a national-security operation — a fact Okafor finds endlessly delicious. His arc is dignity: Book 3 hands him one moment where being a good boss *is* operational security.

### Priya Chandra — 29. Scheduler/dispatcher. The radar.
Four years at the desk; putting her brother Dev through Green River (canon); studying nights for her aircraft-dispatcher certificate (new: gives her arc teeth). Notices everything — Book 2's manifests included. Proposed line (per bible): she assembles the truth herself in Book 3 and handles it so professionally that Okafor offers her a job, horrifying Wren and delighting everyone else. Voice: teasing, fiercely loyal, reads averted eyes like weather depictions (canon).

### Dale Voss — 58. Check airman. Retired Air Branch.
Army rotary → fixed-wing → fifteen years flying "government charters" nobody names (Air Branch, 1990s–2000s) → DPE/check airman with a sideline: for twenty years he has quietly graded pilots for Adaeze. "Adequate" was always a recruitment mark (canon retrofit). The pen clicks are real Morse — dit-dit: *good* (Book 2 reveal, played once, perfectly). Function: the standards conscience of both worlds; trains the crew's emergency work; delivers the series' driest wisdom ("Radio failures don't heal on silence" — canon).

### Manny Ortiz — 45. Mechanic.
IA with a father's pride in the airplane; kid with braces on the company plan (canon). Book 3: the teardown that proves sabotage — his testimony, delivered with a torque wrench in hand, is unimpeachable precisely because he's the least political man alive.

### The ghosts
- **Gus Hale** — the envelope, the E6-B, "one of us should get to look down at the water." Never flew with her. Everything Wren does for Mara is his unfinished flight.
- **Ellen Marsh** — the doorframe knock, the hundred and forty frames of clouds, "now we have a truck that sees over the weather." Jack's compass for what honest love looks like: patient, observant, never performed.
- **Bernard Okafor** — the file shown before the proposal; the proof the series keeps offering that truth and tradecraft can share a house — at a price someone always pays.
- **Danny Ruiz** — see full dossier below. Through Books 1–2 he remains exactly this: weather, off-stage, the reason for rules one and two. ⚠ Book 3 promotes him to the flesh.

### Jax Calloway — 47. The useful idiot.
Pacific Corporate fleet captain; was in the Cascade crew room and said nothing (canon — the sin of the bystander, not the villain). Vain, competent, transactional; recruits for his outfit like it's a lifestyle brand. ⚠ Books 2–3: his charter arm's new owners use him precisely because he repeats things at parties; his gala gossip blows an operational detail he never understood he had. Endgame: not punished by the plot so much as *seen* — Okafor's freeze-out (canon) plus the dawning, career-ending knowledge of what he almost caused. A Clancy world needs one man whose only ideology is his own reflection.

## ⚠ THE BOOK 3 TRAITOR (approved)

**Elliot Kang — 49. BRIDGEBELL logistics coordinator (introduced mid-Book 2 as a friendly).** Twenty years Coast Guard (C-130 loadmaster, then logistics chief, Kodiak and Sacramento); recruited by Okafor 2023 to run ground support — fuel contracts, customs brokers, FBO relationships. Warm, rumpled, competent; the guy who always has the crew car warmed up. The lever: his younger brother **Daniel**, a merchant-marine engineer, arrested on manufactured charges when his ship called at Vladivostok in 2025. The offer came through a lawyer in Seoul: schedules and tail numbers — never lives, they promised — for Daniel's treatment, then his release. Elliot feeds "harmless" logistics data for a year, telling himself it's parking information… until the sabotage, which he never agreed to and cannot undo. His unmasking is Book 3's saddest scene, not its most hateful — the book's thesis in reverse: *he lied to protect someone he loved, one compromise at a time — the road Wren refused.* Resolution: he turns himself in through the crew, testifies, takes his consequences; Daniel comes home in the epilogue's background.

**His handler is Danny Ruiz.**

## ⚠ DANNY RUIZ — 44. Cascade's golden captain. The mole-runner. (Book 3 antagonist — approved)

### Timeline
- **2018–2021** — Cascade Air Group's celebrated captain; charming upward, corrosive downward; pencil-whips training and duty records because rules, in his cosmology, are for people without talent. Burns Wren rather than face the FSDO.
- **2021–2024** — the slide he can't feel happening: a tail-number transfer dressed as promotion, then a Gulf-adjacent operator, then a Part 91 gig flying a leased Gulfstream for shell-company owners who ask no questions and pay in wires. Each rung down confirms his belief that the system is rigged — never that he is the rigging.
- **2025** — the shell companies turn out to have owners of their own. The network that quietly bought Pacific Corporate's charter arm identifies him in an afternoon: a disgraced American charter captain with industry access, expensive habits, a grievance narrative, and practice at falsifying exactly the kinds of records they need falsified. His recruitment is barely a seduction; they simply describe the job as consulting and pay him what he believes he was always worth.
- **2026–Book 3** — courier and spotter work first; then, when Verin's directorate needs a local hand for the Kang channel, Ruiz becomes the cut-out: the "lawyer's" successor, the voice that collects Elliot's schedules and pays in hope about Daniel. He never learns Verin's name. He is a contractor to the end — which is the point of him.

### Function in Book 3
- **He runs the mole:** Kang's handler — meets, taskings, payments; the tradecraft is Verin's, the face is Ruiz's.
- **He authors the misdirection:** when BRIDGEBELL's mole-hunt begins, the network feeds it Wren — and the feed is expert, because Ruiz wrote the original Cascade lie and knows precisely which entries in her history read two ways ("has falsified official documents under pressure" — the Book 1 manifest — is his suggestion). The institution's file on Wren was always half his authorship; in Book 3 he gets to finish the job, and nearly does.
- **He informs the sabotage:** the network's plan against the G550 is built on his professional knowledge of 135 maintenance culture — where a contactor lives, what a busy shop defers, what a crew trusts.
- **The confrontation:** not a gunfight — an interview. Endgame gives Wren fifteen minutes across a table from him (federal building, counsel present) and the discovery that he is *small*: no ideology, no plan, just grievance compounding at interest. She walks out unburdened, because the man who defined three years of her life turns out never to have understood a single thing about her. He goes to prison believing, to the last, that everyone else was doing it too.

### Guardrails
He and Verin never meet on the page; the professional despises the contractor he uses. Ruiz gets no redemption beat and no cartoon menace — his danger is exactly the banal competence of a man who knows how the industry works and owes it nothing. The reader's satisfaction is Wren's: not vengeance — *diagnosis*.
