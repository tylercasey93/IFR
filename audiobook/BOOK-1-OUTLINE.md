# BOOK 1 — *WINTER HARBOR* (working title) — Chapter Outline v2

*Implements [SERIES-BIBLE.md](SERIES-BIBLE.md) v7/v8. Twenty-one chapters, ~84,000 words (≈4,000/chapter), sized for a 9–10 hour audiobook. **Status: approved structure — now in execution.***

## Narration rules (bible decisions 19–20)

- **First person, one POV per chapter**, named at the chapter head.
- **Four narrators, unequal shares.** Jack (8 chapters) and Cole (8) carry the book and alternate as the default. **Wren (3) and Okafor (2) take chapters from the beginning** — not on a rotation, but wherever the story can only move forward from their seat: the thriller thread the pilots can't see, the assessment they don't know they're taking, and the two moments when someone has to watch these men from outside.
- **Chronological throughout.** Act I runs about five weeks of fall 2026; from Chapter 9 the book runs on a mission clock (*Nine Days* … *Day Six*), carried in every chapter head. No structural flashbacks — backstory arrives only as memory or dialogue inside a moving scene.
- **Clancy calibration:** every chapter advances the clock through procedure rendered lovingly. The narrator is a professional walking you through professional work, and the ACS teaching rides inside that walkthrough. Cut between threads for pressure, never for decoration.
- Standing policies survive: flying is flying, love is spoken plainly; the Crew Debrief ritual closes most chapters and becomes a dual flying/tradecraft debrief once the op is live.

## ACT I — THE CREW (ch. 1–8, ~5 weeks)

**1. Meridian Five-Fifty** — *Jack.*
A pre-dawn Okafor leg out of Boeing Field introduces everything through Jack's cool, evaluating eye: the G550 and its too-quiet owner trust, Griff's leaking company, Priya's radar, and the captain — two years, one cockpit, the best friend he's ever had and the only person his polish has never worked on. A contract fill-in pilot's currency doesn't survive Cole's audit (*I.A — currency vs. proficiency, 61.57, 66-HIT, the 135 check ladder*). The FBO desk assumes Jack is the captain again; his correction is instant. Texture: Aaron; the glareshield double-tap. Debrief ritual established.

**2. The Routing Request** — *Cole.*
Okafor asks for a routing that makes no commercial sense — her second confirmation test, which the reader may clock and Cole doesn't — and Cole builds the weather picture the way his father read the Blues (*I.B — METAR/TAF, TEMPO vs. PROB30, AIRMETs/SIGMETs, PIREPs as ground truth*). Interior: warmth as perimeter; everyone on the field likes him and nobody has been inside since Cascade. Elias texture. Static: a "journalist" has been calling around about Cascade.

**3. Changi** — *Wren.* **(The thriller thread opens.)**
Eight thousand miles away, a woman the crew has never heard of runs from a conference hotel with a teenager and two suitcases, and BRIDGEBELL's contract aviator flies the first hop out — a light jet, a legend, and paperwork that has to be perfect at four hours' notice (*international bonus — ICAO flight plans, customs and general-declaration reality, crossing borders on documents that must survive a bored professional's second look*). Wren's competence, her flatness, and her rule about not learning passengers' names — broken inside an hour by a fifteen-year-old who asks what the airplane is called. Ends with Sorokina and Mara delivered to Sapporo, and Wren's report to Okafor: *this hide holds for days, not weeks.*

**4. Lodge Investors** — *Jack.*
A Southeast Alaska charter for Okafor's "lodge investment" associates — the cover business being aged in plain sight. Jack plans the leg and narrates the craft (*I.C — the 1-2-3 rule, 600-2/800-2, alternate selection, 45-minute fuel, the NOTAM with your name on it*). Over the water, Okafor's biography questions run one layer too precise, and Jack — a connoisseur of technique — notices technique without knowing what he's looking at. Static: someone is probing Griff's debts.

**5. The Trust** — *Cole.*
Maintenance day with Manny; the airplane opened up (*II.A — pitot-static and glass architecture, bleed-air anti-ice versus the boots of his freight years, electrical redundancy*). Rainier Aviation Holdings pays late again and stipulates oddly again; Cole pulls the trust paperwork and finds it perfectly, disquietingly clean. Sam Marsh cameo at the field: easy warmth, the hospital corridor underneath it unmentioned. Interior: rule one, and how carefully he avoids examining why he needs it.

**6. The No-Go** — *Jack.*
Loaded, catered, principal aboard — and the standby attitude indicator is dead (*II.B — 91.205(d), the MEL versus 91.213 decision tree; "standby's required equipment; no relief on that item"*). Cole scrubs the trip with the whole account watching. At the salvaged dinner afterward, Jack registers that their client is not disappointed — she is *pleased* — and files it wrong. Aaron beat: a shift-wrecked evening, kind and weightless, establishing what Jack thinks he wants.

**7. After Tea** — *Okafor.* **(The reader gets ahead of the crew.)**
Her side, at last: the assessment file with two pilots in it, Voss's twenty-year grades, the hotels that were always also infrastructure — and the cable that ruins her week, because the route that was going to bring Sorokina home has collapsed and every alternative is watched. The chapter is a woman doing arithmetic she hates: nine days, one clean airplane, two men she has come to love a little, and the certainty that asking will cost at least one of them something permanent. Ends with her booking the conference room for eight a.m. and pouring the tea she makes no decisions before.

**8. Icing** — *Cole.*
The coastal trip that proves the crew: forecast SLD, an escape brief, the encounter, the diversion flown like choreography (*I.B applied — icing products, escape doctrine; the origin of his minimums card told in-scene*). Okafor aboard, silent, finished deciding. Act I closes on the crew at its best, and on Priya relaying a message the reader already understands and Cole does not.

## THE HINGE

**9. The Briefing Room** — *Jack. (Nine Days.)*
The mask off: BRIDGEBELL, Sorokina, Mara, Sapporo, nine days — the truth, the stakes, and the door, offered once, now, with no consequence. Jack, who has spent his life being the most composed man in every room, watches someone be better at it and feels the first real pull of the work. Ends on Cole's only question: *"Who signs the manifest?"* (*Light teaching: 91.3 and command authority; what a Part 135 certificate is, and why boring paperwork is the whole disguise.*)

## ACT II — PREPARATION (ch. 10–14)

**10. The Third Pilot** — *Cole. (Eight Days.)*
Wren arrives with the legend paperwork and a fiancé-shaped hole in it that Okafor has already decided Jack fills. Cole runs the airplane's readiness check alongside her (*II.C — the instrument flight deck check, rendered as two guarded professionals sizing each other up through a checklist*) and recognizes the profile: another sealed archive. The manifest war begins. So does something else, which he files, expertly, under nothing.

**11. Tradecraft** — *Jack. (Seven Days.)*
Couple school, taught deadpan: backstory drills, ring logistics, how couples stand at a check-in desk, where your eyes go when she laughs. Jack is flawless immediately — and quieter every day, because every gesture he manufactures is a copy of one he already makes, unthinking, for the man across the briefing room. Aaron's exit lands here, kind and surgical. (*III.A — CRAFT, readback/hearback, and the ICAO format the mission demands: legend-building and clearance-copying taught as the same discipline — say exactly what you mean, omit nothing, accept only what you can fly.*)

**12. The Jumpseat** — *Wren. (Six Days.)*
Voss's "recurrent" sims, which are obviously rehearsal, narrated from the jumpseat by the only person in the building with no stake in either man (*III.B — holding: the 70-degree line, entries, speeds, EFCs, "no EFC means ask"; IV.A — attitude instrument flying and the scan's fragile democracy*). Evening: the legend's first public outing, Jack and Wren performing while Cole attends as the colleague — and Wren, six legends deep, watches a captain discover that there is weather he has no escape brief for. Her verdict to Jack in the elevator: *"You're the first one who keeps checking whether someone else is watching."*

**13. The Signature** — *Cole. (Four Days.)*
Unusual-attitude work in the box (*IV.B — recognition and recovery, taught against the icing of trust: believe the instruments, not the body*). Then the chapter the book has been aimed at: Cole and Okafor alone, the manifest question fought to the floor, the Ruiz story told entire — the two years, the forged initials, the smear. Her answer, and his decision, made with open eyes and filed where no one will see the cost. Elias's exit: in person, decent, unexplained.

**14. Two Days** — *Jack. (Two Days.)*
The final mission brief rendered with love (*international bonus — eAPIS, customs brokers, oceanic clearance strategy, fuel criticality, the logic of the Anchorage tech stop*). Sunday dinner with Sam: the first table in Jack's life he has to lie at, and Sam watches him do it and asks only, on the porch, whether Cole is going along. Midnight in the crew room over the charts — the almost-moment, interrupted by op traffic.

## ACT III — THE RUN (ch. 15–20)

**15. Northbound** — *Cole. (Day One.)*
KBFI–PANC, the mission airborne (*RVSM and high-altitude ops — the flight levels as a rules regime, oxygen doctrine, coffin-corner respect; departure gradient math off Boeing Field*). The investor party aboard, the legend live, Jack and Wren playing bored money in the cabin while Cole flies with a manifest in his bag that is, for now, still true. Anchorage: fuel, customs choreography, and a ramp agent's question one degree too specific.

**16. Oceanic** — *Jack. (Day Two.)*
PANC–RJCC: position reports, HF and CPDLC, the discipline of flying where nobody can see you (*III.A applied — oceanic clearance and the promise-keeping architecture of non-radar separation: fly what you said, when you said it*). Descent into Hokkaido; the check-in performance; one suite, of course. The first night of the op spent four feet from a woman he is fake-engaged to, texting the captain across the hall about nothing, both of them narrating everything except the one thing.

**17. Sapporo** — *Okafor. (Day Three — the dwell day, collapsing.)*
Her hotel, her asset, her arithmetic. The window caves in by hours as the sweep runs ahead of schedule; a spotter photographs the Gulfstream at the FBO. Okafor watches Cole and Sorokina meet in a staff corridor and recognize each other in about ninety seconds — two people who ended careers over falsified purposes — and understands exactly what she is about to spend. Launch moves to tomorrow, papers or no papers.

**18. The Pickup** — *Wren. (Day Four.)*
Off-script from the first minute: Mara's papers aren't ready, the headcount changes twice, and two human beings are folded into the investor party in a service elevator with Verin's grid one phone call behind. Then the departure hold, narrated from the jumpseat (*III.B applied — entry off the 70-degree line, EFC arithmetic, fuel doing the talking*), Jack working the radios in two languages and one legend while Wren watches two men fly an airplane the way other people finish sentences. Mara's verdict on Jack from the back: *"You are like a spy from a movie, except sad."*

**19. The Aleutian Option** — *Cole. (Day Four, the long night.)*
The chain, the weather closing, the searchers a half-step behind, and the book's diversion set piece (*I.C applied for survival — alternate math, fuel criticality, a gravel-adjacent Alaska alternate briefed and flown; "the NOTAM you skip has your name on it," cashed at full value*). In cruise, Cole takes out the manifest and signs it: one paragraph, no music. That night, a bunkhouse that smells like diesel, a dual debrief, and eleven things unsaid.

**20. Entry** — *Jack. (Day Five.)*
The last leg and the arrival where the paperwork must be perfect *and* false: customs, eAPIS reality, the longest twenty minutes of the book conducted entirely in normalcy. The asset delivered; the handoff; the ramp emptying. Then the kiss — nobody performing, nobody watching. That night, the last shared room of the operation, they stop pretending. Written to the established heat standard: warm, open-door, human language, and not one word of definition.

**21. Debrief** — *Cole. (Day Six.)*
The morning after, executed with catastrophic professionalism by two fluent communicators declining to have a conversation. The formal debrief; Sorokina and Mara's new beginning glimpsed; Wren's exit line at the curb — *"Best fake fiancé I've had. Tell him, or I will."* — delivered to Jack and overheard by exactly one person. Okafor's offer: not a mission this time, a *job*. Double cliffhanger: the offer unanswered, the night undiscussed. Final image: Cole alone with his minimums card, adding a line that has nothing to do with weather, then crossing it out.

## Execution protocol (author in the loop)

Chapters are drafted with the author steering **several times per chapter**, not once at the end:

1. **Pre-draft call** — before writing, the chapter's live choices go to the author (opening image, whose scene carries the teaching, which relationship beat fires, anything that forks the book).
2. **Movement check** — the chapter's first movement (~1,200–1,500 words) is delivered for direction before the rest is written.
3. **Mid-chapter fork** — at the chapter's real decision point (the line of dialogue that changes the book, the beat that could go two ways), the author calls it.
4. **Delivered draft** — the full chapter, with a short note on what it set up and what the next chapter inherits.

Author decisions are logged as numbered entries in the series bible so nothing gets re-litigated.
