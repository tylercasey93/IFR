# BOOK 1 — *WINTER HARBOR* (working title) — Chapter Outline v1

*Implements [SERIES-BIBLE.md](SERIES-BIBLE.md) v7. Twenty chapters, ~60k words, sized for a 9–10 hour audiobook. This is the outline for approval — no prose is written until it's signed off.*

## Narration rules (per bible decision 19)

- **First person, one POV per chapter**, marked at the chapter head: **Jack takes odd chapters, Cole takes even ones.** Jack opens the book; Cole closes it.
- **Chronological throughout.** Act I covers about five weeks of fall 2026; from Chapter 7 the story runs on a mission clock (*Nine Days* … *Day Zero*), and every chapter head after the unmasking carries the clock.
- **Clancy calibration:** each chapter advances the clock through procedure rendered lovingly — the narrator is a professional walking you through professional work, and the ACS teaching lives inside that walkthrough, never beside it. Backstory arrives only as memory or dialogue inside a moving scene.
- Standing policies survive: flying is flying, love is spoken plainly; Crew Debrief closes most chapters (it becomes a dual flying/tradecraft debrief once the op is live).

## ACT I — THE CREW (ch. 1–6, ~5 weeks)

**1. Meridian Five-Fifty** — *Jack.*
A dawn Okafor leg out of Boeing Field introduces everything through Jack's cool, evaluating eye: the G550 and its too-quiet owner trust, Griff's leaking company, Priya's radar, and the captain — two years, one cockpit, the best friend he's ever had and the one person his polish has never worked on. A contract fill-in pilot's paperwork doesn't survive Cole's audit (*teaching: I.A — currency vs. proficiency, 61.57, 66-HIT, the 135 check ladder*), and the FBO desk assumes Jack is the captain again; his correction is instant. Texture: Aaron, the easy arrangement; the glareshield double-tap. Debrief ritual established.

**2. The Routing Request** — *Cole.*
Okafor asks for a routing that makes no commercial sense (her second confirmation test — the reader may clock it; Cole doesn't), and Cole builds the weather picture the way his father read the Blues (*teaching: I.B — METAR/TAF, TEMPO vs. PROB30, AIRMETs/SIGMETs, PIREPs as ground truth*). Interior: warmth as perimeter — everyone on the field likes him; nobody has been inside since Cascade. Elias texture: dinner scheduled, contained, safe. Static seed: a "journalist" has been calling around about Cascade.

**3. Lodge Investors** — *Jack.*
A Southeast Alaska charter for Okafor's "lodge investment" associates — the cover business being aged in plain sight, though no one knows it yet. Jack plans the leg and narrates the craft (*teaching: I.C — the 1-2-3 rule, 600-2/800-2, alternate selection, 45-minute fuel, NOTAMs with your name on them*). Over the water, Okafor's biography questions get one layer too precise; Jack, a connoisseur of technique, notices technique. Wren does not appear — but this chapter's route is the one she'll later ride. Static: someone is probing Griff's debts.

**4. The Trust** — *Cole.*
Maintenance day with Manny; the G550 opened up (*teaching: II.A — pitot-static and vacuum-free glass, bleed-air anti-ice vs. the boots of Cole's freight years, electrical redundancy*). Rainier Aviation Holdings pays late again, stipulates oddly again; Cole pulls the trust paperwork and finds it perfectly, disquietingly clean. Sam Marsh cameo at the field — Cole and Sam's easy warmth, the corridor history underneath it unmentioned. Interior: rule one, and how carefully he doesn't look at why he needs it.

**5. The No-Go** — *Jack.*
Loaded, catered, client aboard — and the standby attitude indicator is dead (*teaching: II.B — 91.205(d), the MEL versus 91.213 decision tree, "standby's required; no relief on that item"*). Cole scrubs the trip with the whole account watching; Okafor's third test, and her last. At the recovery dinner she watches them run the failure as one organism and makes her decision on-page (the reader sees it; they don't). Aaron beat: a shift-wrecked evening that's kind and weightless — establishing what Jack thinks he wants.

**6. Icing** — *Cole.*
The coastal trip that proves the crew: forecast SLD, an escape brief, the encounter, the diversion flown like choreography (*teaching: I.B applied — icing products, escape doctrine; the Zulu-winter DNA of Cole's minimums card, origin story told in-scene*). Okafor aboard, silent, done deciding. The chapter ends Act I on the crew at its best — and on Priya relaying a message: Ms. Okafor has canceled the week's schedule and booked the conference room for eight a.m.

## THE HINGE

**7. The Briefing Room** — *Jack. (Nine Days.)*
The mask comes off: the hotels that were always also infrastructure, BRIDGEBELL, and the ask — Dr. Yelena Sorokina and her daughter, staff quarters of a Sapporo hotel, a service watching manifests and borders, nine days. The truth, the stakes, and the door: anyone may walk, once, now, no consequence. Jack — who has spent his life being the most composed man in every room — watches Okafor be better at it, and feels the first pull of the work. The chapter ends on Cole's only question: *"Who signs the manifest?"* (*Teaching: light — 91.3 and command authority; what a Part 135 certificate actually is, and why its boring paperwork is the whole disguise.*)

## ACT II — PREPARATION (ch. 8–13)

**8. The Third Pilot** — *Cole. (Eight Days.)*
Wren Hale arrives with the legend paperwork and a fiancé-shaped hole in it that Okafor has already decided Jack fills. Cole runs the airplane's op-readiness check with her (*teaching: II.C — the instrument flight deck check, rendered as two professionals sizing each other up via checklist*) and recognizes the profile: another integrity file, another set of sealed archives. Interior: the manifest war begins — and something else begins when the fake-dating assignment is briefed, which he files, expertly, under nothing.

**9. Tradecraft** — *Jack. (Seven Days.)*
Couple school, taught deadpan by Wren: backstory drills, ring logistics, how couples stand at a check-in desk, where your eyes go when she laughs. Jack is flawless immediately — and quieter every day, because every gesture he manufactures is a copy of something he already does, unthinking, for the man watching from across the briefing room. Aaron's exit lands here, kind and surgical: *"It wouldn't be her I was losing to anyway."* (*Teaching: III.A — CRAFT, readback/hearback discipline, and the ICAO flight-plan format the international legs will demand — legend-building and clearance-copying taught as the same skill: say exactly what you mean, omit nothing, accept only what you can fly.*)

**10. Recurrent** — *Cole. (Six Days.)*
Voss appears "coincidentally" for recurrent sims that are obviously rehearsal (*teaching: III.B — holding: the 70-degree line, entries, speeds, EFCs, "no EFC means ask"*). Evening: a public legend outing — Jack and Wren performing couplehood at a gala-adjacent function, Cole attending as the colleague — and Cole discovers that watching is a kind of weather he has no escape brief for. Elias's exit: in person, decent, unexplained. *"Figured. Good luck, Captain."*

**11. Partial Truth** — *Jack. (Five Days.)*
Sim day, Voss on the panel (*teaching: IV.A — attitude instrument flying, the scan's fragile democracy, roll-before-pull; the vestibular liar taught through a demonstration that humbles even Jack*). Night: the legend's first performed kiss, staged for a lobby camera — technically perfect, and it lands wrong in his chest like a mis-set altimeter. Wren clocks him in the elevator afterward: *"I've done six of these. You're the first one who keeps checking whether someone else is watching."* Jack, for the first time on-page, has no reply.

**12. The Signature** — *Cole. (Four Days.)*
Unusual-attitude work in the box (*teaching: IV.B — recognition and recovery, taught against the icing of trust: believe the instruments, not the body*). Then the chapter the book has been building to: Cole and Okafor, alone, and the manifest question fought to the floor — Ruiz's story told to the reader entire, the two years, the forged initials, the smear. Her answer — *Ruiz lied to protect himself; this protects someone else, and you sign your own name* — and his decision, made with open eyes, its cost filed where no one will see it. He tells the crew he's in. He tells no one what it cost.

**13. Two Days** — *Jack. (Two Days.)*
Final mission brief rendered with love (*teaching: international ops — eAPIS, customs brokers, oceanic clearance strategy, ETOPS-adjacent fuel criticality, the Anchorage tech stop's logic*). Sunday dinner with Sam — the first table in Jack's life he has to lie at, and Sam watches him do it and asks only, on the porch, whether Cole is going along. ("Yeah." — "Good.") Packing; a crew-room almost-moment at midnight over the charts, interrupted by op traffic — the phone that saves them both, or costs them both, depending which narrator you ask.

## ACT III — THE RUN (ch. 14–19)

**14. Northbound** — *Cole. (Day One.)*
KBFI–PANC, the mission airborne at last (*teaching: RVSM and high-altitude ops — the flight levels as a rules regime, oxygen doctrine, coffin-corner respect; departure procedures and gradient math out of Boeing Field*). The investor party aboard, the legend live, Jack and Wren playing bored money in the cabin while Cole flies with a manifest in his flight bag that is still, for now, true. Anchorage tech stop: fuel, customs choreography rehearsal, and the first grain of static — a ramp agent's question one degree too specific.

**15. Oceanic** — *Jack. (Day Two.)*
PANC–RJCC: position reports, HF and CPDLC texture, the discipline of flying where radar can't see you (*teaching: III.A applied — oceanic clearances, the promise-keeping architecture of non-radar separation: fly what you said, when you said it*). Descent into Hokkaido; the check-in performance — one suite, of course; and the first night of the op spent four feet from a stranger he's fake-engaged to, texting the captain across the hall about nothing, both of them narrating everything except the one thing.

**16. Sapporo** — *Cole. (Day Three — the dwell day, collapsing.)*
Jack and Wren tour "lodge sites" in performance; Cole runs the airplane, the contingencies, and the weather while the timeline caves in by hours — the sweep is ahead of schedule, and the safe window is shrinking. First meeting with Sorokina in the staff corridor: two people who ended careers over falsified purposes recognize each other in about ninety seconds (*teaching seed: she looks at their green-needle backup plan and says it's the only part her old directorate would respect*). A spotter photographs the Gulfstream at the FBO. The launch moves to tomorrow, papers or no papers.

**17. The Pickup** — *Jack. (Day Four.)*
Off-script from the first minute: Mara's papers aren't ready, the party's headcount changes twice, and the swap — two human beings folded into the investor legend in a service elevator — happens with Verin's grid one phone call behind. Departure with an ATC hold thrown on top for weather-and-traffic (*teaching: III.B applied — a real hold, entry off the 70-degree line, EFC arithmetic, fuel talking*), Jack flying the radios in two languages and one legend. Airborne, finally, with the two passengers who do not exist — and Mara's verdict on him from the back: *"You are like a spy from a movie, except sad."*

**18. The Aleutian Option** — *Cole. (Day Four, the long night.)*
The return across the chain into deteriorating weather, the searchers a half-step behind, and the book's diversion set piece (*teaching: I.C applied for survival — the alternate math, fuel criticality, the gravel-adjacent Alaska alternate briefed and flown; "the NOTAM you skip has your name on it" cashed at full value*). En route, in cruise, Cole takes out the manifest and signs it — the pen-on-paper moment the whole book has aimed at, one paragraph, no music. Debrief that night in a bunkhouse that smells like diesel: flying debrief, tradecraft debrief, and eleven things unsaid.

**19. Entry** — *Jack. (Day Five.)*
The last leg home and the arrival where the paperwork must be perfect *and* false: customs, eAPIS reality, the longest twenty minutes of the book conducted entirely in normalcy (*teaching: international arrival procedure — flown, not lectured*). The asset delivered; the handoff; the ramp emptying out. And then the kiss — Jack and Cole, on the ramp at the end of the worst leg, nobody performing, nobody watching. That night, the last shared room of the operation, they stop pretending — written to the established heat standard: warm, open-door, human language, and not one word of definition.

## AFTERMATH

**20. Debrief** — *Cole. (Day Six.)*
The morning after, executed with catastrophic professionalism by two fluent communicators declining to have a conversation. The formal debrief; Sorokina and Mara's new beginning glimpsed; Wren's exit line on her way to the curb — *"Best fake fiancé I've had. Tell him, or I will."* — delivered to Jack, overheard by no one, which is to say: overheard by Cole. Okafor's offer: not a mission this time, a *job*, laid on the table before a crew she can plainly see has crossed its own border. Double cliffhanger, per the bible: the offer unanswered, the night undiscussed. Final image: Cole alone with his minimums card, adding a line to it that has nothing to do with weather — then crossing it out.
