# CLEARED AS FILED — Story Bible

A fake-dating-to-real-dating romance set in the modern Part 135 charter world, engineered so that every chapter teaches one Area of Operation from the Instrument Rating ACS. Companion to the [ACS Correlation Wiki](../wiki/README.md).

## Format & voice

- **Length target:** 20 chapters, 3,800–4,800 words each (≈85k words; ≈9–10 hours narrated at 150 wpm).
- **POV:** Close third person, alternating by chapter — **Wren** (odd chapters) and **Theo** (even chapters). Each chapter opens by naming the POV character in the first line so an audio listener never loses track.
- **Heat level:** Warm, mainstream romance. Banter-forward slow burn; two tasteful open-door scenes at the big beats (Ch 13 and Ch 19); fade discreetly rather than explicit.
- **Tense:** Past.
- **Audio-friendly rules:** Short scene breaks announced with a beat ("Later, in the FBO lobby—"), no long unattributed dialogue runs, numbers spoken naturally ("ninety-one one eighty-five" for 91.185 on second reference).
- **Crew Debrief:** Every chapter ends with a 100–200 word in-story recap — one character quizzing the other — that restates the chapter's ACS content plainly. This is the retention engine.

## Premise

**Meridian Air Charter**, a struggling Part 135 operator at Boeing Field (KBFI), Seattle. Two-pilot **King Air 350** (N135MA, callsign "Meridian Three Five"). Meridian's survival hangs on one whale client: **Adaeze Okafor**, a hotel magnate who charters weekly up and down the West Coast — and who, after catching captain and first officer mid-argument that looked like a lovers' quarrel, decided they were a couple, declared them her lucky crew, and made "the couple" a condition of the contract. Meridian's owner begs them to play along for one season.

## Voice & dynamic (revised — Fox/O'Hare calibration)

Character chemistry modeled on Evanovich & Goldberg's **Fox and O'Hare** pairing: her exasperated competence vs. his delighted charm, comedy-forward sparring, and a very slow burn — attraction expressed as antagonism and denial for the first half of the book, tenderness strictly rationed and always paid for.

**Slow-burn pacing contract:** Ch 1–7 = sparring, denial, respect accruing grudgingly; feelings appear only as irritation, tells, and things not said. Ch 8 = adrenaline kiss, instantly and aggressively denied. Ch 9–12 = denial era with leaks (deniable jabs, interrupted almosts). Ch 13 = the turn. Ch 14+ = together, banter intact. Personal reveals are rationed late: Theo's glareshield tap stays a mystery until Ch 8; his mother lands fully in Ch 10; Wren's father's story lands in Ch 9; the shoebox stays sealed until Ch 19.

## Cast

- **Captain Wren Hale, 34** — the Kate O'Hare of the pairing. Meridian's youngest captain, ex-freight dog (Chieftains in Alaska). Blunt, deadpan, physically capable, threatens Theo with cargo manifests and Yakima on a regular basis, and considers charm a known icing condition. Motto: *"Current isn't proficient."* Secret vices: peanut M&Ms stashed in her flight bag (denied under questioning) and a shoebox of expired charts (denied under torture). Guarded since a former captain-boyfriend torched her reputation at Cascade. Feelings emerge strictly as irritation first.
- **First Officer Theo Marsh, 31** — the Nick Fox of the pairing. Former CFII and showman ground instructor; walked away from a regional airline class date after his father's stroke. Unflappable, shamelessly charming, regards the fake-dating scheme as the best hand he's ever been dealt, and cannot resist embellishing their cover story in front of the client purely to watch Wren corroborate it while planning his death. Deploys "sweetheart" tactically. Flirts by explaining regulations. The sincerity underneath is real and revealed on a strict ration.
- **Chidinma, 26** — Okafor's goddaughter, a London commercial-fraud barrister who flies out specifically to run diligence on the suspiciously charming crew. The story's professional skeptic: pressure-tests the cover story (Ch 7), delivers the "nobody can fake crew" verdict (Ch 12), and quietly re-runs the diligence when it matters (Ch 18).
- **Running gags:** Theo's escalating cover-story embellishments (the orcas, and Wren's retaliatory teddy bear, Bernoulli); Wren's threats ("I will list you as cargo"; the lav-cart contingency plan for Jax) which visibly *nourish* him; Wren's food doctrine (peanut M&Ms double freezer-bagged as "ballast" from Ch 1, the logger's special, the gala canapé forward operating base — "it's not an appetite, it's doctrine"); Theo rating everything like a critic (FBO stars — Pendleton five, Santa Barbara docked for cologne) and naming his adversaries (Gary the GPS jammer); "documented occurrences" (Wren's laugh count); Voss's pen clicks; "adequate" as highest praise; the fictional orca catchphrase Wren later says for real (Ch 3 → Ch 6); banter under fire as the crew's pressure valve (Ch 18's breakfast promise).
- **Adaeze Okafor, 60s** — The client. Sharp, romantic, impossible to lie to (dramatic irony: audiences sense she knows). Asks the questions that force fake intimacy: how did you meet, who reached for whom first.
- **Marcus "Griff" Griffin, 58** — Meridian's owner/DO. Good pilot, terrible businessman. Set the fake-dating scheme in motion; increasingly guilty about it.
- **Priya Chandra, 29** — Meridian's scheduler/dispatch. Wren's confidante. Ships it openly.
- **Dale Voss** — Check airman contracted for 135.293/297 checks. Dry as a NOTAM. Appears for training/check events (the excuse for oral-exam-style scenes).
- **Sam Marsh** — Theo's father, recovering stroke patient, retired 727 flight engineer. Grounds Theo's backstory; BasicMed subplot for his Bonanza.

## Why the ACS content is diegetic

- Theo is prepping for his **captain upgrade** (135.293/297 orals + type-of-check content ≈ instrument ACS oral) — Wren drills him; he counter-drills her because teaching is how he flirts.
- Wren mentors a part-time SIC/instrument student thread and flies with Voss for recurrent checks.
- Okafor loves listening to crew briefings on the jump-seat speaker and demands "the story of the flight" — the excuse for narrated planning scenes.

## Flying/romance separation policy (revised by author direction)

**The flying is the flying; the romance is human.** The plot may *rhyme* with the aviation concepts at the structural level (the breakup falls in the lost-comm chapter; the reconciliation happens during an emergency), but the characters do not court each other in jargon:

- Declarations, confessions, apologies, the Pendleton night, and the proposal are spoken in **plain human language** — no clearances, callouts, checklist formats, or radio phraseology as romantic dialogue.
- In-world contract terms the characters wrote themselves survive as plot mechanics: the operating agreement, its clauses, and "going missed" as the agreed exit word.
- Light pilot-flavored banter is allowed in *low-stakes* moments (it's how these people joke), but the bigger the emotional beat, the plainer the words.
- Narration may carry an occasional structural echo (the title motif "cleared as filed"), used sparingly.

| Structural rhyme (allowed) | Jargon romance (removed) |
|---|---|
| The breakup lands in the lost-comm chapter; Wren copes by "staying findable" | Proposals/confessions delivered in CRAFT/readback format |
| The reconciliation happens mid-emergency because crisis burns off the misunderstanding | "Cleared for the approach" as an invitation; cockpit callouts as intimacy |
| The agreement's clauses drive the plot | Metaphor-duel conversations (holding/EFC, DME-arc, DA/red-bars) as courtship |

## Continuity ledger (final, as written)

- Aircraft: King Air 350, N135MA; "Meridian Three-Five." Boeing Field (KBFI) home base. Sam's V35B Bonanza (IO-550) carries the BasicMed subplot. A second King Air arrives in Ch 20.
- Theo drives a blue Subaru with a mismatched fender; Wren's chart-keeping shoebox is revealed to Theo in Ch 19.
- Okafor's standing order: ginger tea, no ice, at top of climb. Her goddaughter Chidinma appears Ch 7, 9, 12, 13.
- The operating agreement (Ch 2): Clause One — no lying to anyone wearing a headset; Clause Two — no performance without an audience (repealed by Amendment One, Ch 13); Clause Three — daily debrief, both directions; Clause Four — either party may "go missed," no questions asked (invoked Ch 16; Amendment Four adds the "confirm" rule, Ch 18). Amendments One–Three ratified in Pendleton (Ch 13).
- Running props: the legal-pad agreement and the coaster reading I'M NOT GOING ANYWHERE live in Wren's left chest jacket pocket (the zipper one); the sealed letter rides there in Ch 17–18; Theo's unread proposal speech (Ch 20); the failed bus-tie contactor becomes a hangar trophy (Ch 19).
- Running gags: Wren counts Theo-induced laughs ("documented occurrences," 1–8+); Voss's pen clicks (two = ovation); "narration is free, assumptions are not"; "adequate" as highest praise; "boring on purpose" for good alternates.
- Recurring phrases with arc weight: "current isn't proficient" (Ch 1 → Ch 20), "we always need the alternate" (Ch 3 → Ch 19), "standby's required equipment — no relief on that item" (Ch 5 → 18), "decide while you're rich" (Ch 13), "keep the lights on" (Ch 17 → 20), "I'm not going anywhere" (Ch 7 coaster → Ch 13), "cleared as filed" (title motif: narration only, plus one Ch 19 joke).
- Cascade Air Group backstory: Captain Danny Ruiz; Jax Calloway (Pacific Corporate Air) is the Ch 11/15/16 complication. Voss gave Wren her 135 checks post-Cascade.
- Timeline: early October through the following May. Gala at the Rainier Club mid-winter; Pasco emergency the following week; proposal at Friday Harbor in May.

## Accuracy guardrails

- Part 135 two-crew ops: both pilots hold instrument ratings and second-class or better medicals — BasicMed content is carried by Sam's Bonanza subplot, not the charter flying.
- All procedures, altitudes, and regs must match the [wiki](../wiki/README.md); when drama needs a rule bent, a character must *notice* it's being bent.
- Real fixes/airports used loosely; frequencies fictionalized.
