## Task A. Checking Instruments and Equipment

**ACS references:** 14 CFR part 91; FAA-H-8083-2, -3, -25; POH/AFM
**Objective:** Checking flight instruments and equipment during postflight.

| ACS Element | Element summary | 14 CFR (FARs) | AIM | IFH 8083-15B | Other refs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| IR.VIII.A.K1 | Documenting in-flight/postflight discrepancies | **91.405(b)** (discrepancy records); 91.417 (maintenance records); 43.9/43.11 (record entries for maintenance & inspections); 91.7(b) (PIC discontinues when unairworthy) | 1-1-13 (report NAVAID outages you observed) | — | POH/AFM; operator squawk sheet |
| IR.VIII.A.R1 | Performing & documenting postflight inspection and discrepancies | 91.405; 91.213 (an open discrepancy may ground the next IFR flight) | — | — | — |
| IR.VIII.A.S1 | Conduct a postflight inspection; document discrepancies & servicing needs | 91.405 | — | — | POH/AFM |

**Study notes**
- The instrument tie-in: a squawk like "glideslope flagged on the ILS" or "AI slow to erect" isn't just maintenance paperwork — under 91.213 / 91.405 it determines whether the **next** flight can legally launch IFR. Write it up so the determination can be made.
- If a NAVAID appeared to malfunction in flight, ATC/FSS want the report (AIM 1-1-13) — pairs with the 91.187 in-flight malfunction report from [Area VII, Task D](Area-VII-Emergency-Operations.md#task-d-approach-with-loss-of-primary-flight-instrument-indicators).
