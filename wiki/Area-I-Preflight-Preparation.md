**Study notes**
- **"66-HIT"**: within the preceding **6** calendar months — **6** instrument approaches, **H**olding, **I**ntercepting & **T**racking (61.57(c)). No hour requirement. Blown past 6 months? You have another 6 months to regain currency with a safety pilot; after that, an IPC (61.57(d)).
- Safety pilot must hold at least a private certificate with appropriate category/class ratings and a current medical, and the aircraft needs dual controls (91.109(c)). BasicMed can qualify a safety pilot only when acting as PIC.

**Study notes**
- **1-2-3 rule** (91.169): no alternate required if, from **1** hour before to **1** hour after ETA, forecast ceiling ≥ **2,000 ft** and visibility ≥ **3 SM** — and the destination has an instrument approach. Otherwise file one.
- Alternate weather is judged **at the time you'd arrive at the alternate**, using the **published alternate minimums** for that airport (TPP), not the approach minimums.
- GPS-only aircraft alternate planning: see AIM 1-1-17/1-1-18 and TPP notes (WAAS users may plan LNAV/circling MDA as a non-precision line at the alternate).

**Study notes**
- **IFR fuel (91.167):** fly to destination, then to the alternate (if required), then **45 minutes** at normal cruising speed. Compare with the 30-minute VFR day rule — instrument students commonly mix these up.
- **IFR cruising altitudes (91.179):** on a magnetic **course** 0°–179° fly **odd** thousands; 180°–359° fly **even** thousands (below FL290, in controlled airspace fly what ATC assigns).
- **Minimum altitudes (91.177):** where no MEA/MOCA published — 2,000 ft above highest obstacle within 4 NM in mountainous terrain, 1,000 ft elsewhere. MOCA guarantees nav signal only within 22 NM of the VOR.
