## Task A. Instrument Flight

**ACS references:** FAA-H-8083-2, -3, -15, -16, -25
**Objective:** Basic flight maneuvers solely by reference to instruments.

This is the most handbook-centric task in the ACS — the FARs and AIM say almost nothing about *how* to fly on the gauges; the Instrument Flying Handbook is the primary text.

**Study notes**
- **Control/Performance method:** establish attitude + power → trim → cross-check → adjust. **Primary/Supporting method:** the instrument giving the most pertinent, direct information for the parameter being controlled is *primary* (e.g., straight-and-level: altimeter primary for pitch, heading indicator primary for bank, ASI primary for power). The IFH Ch 4 teaches both; be able to answer orals in either framework.
- The three classic scan errors (IFH Ch 4): **fixation** (staring), **omission** (skipping), **emphasis** (over-relying on one instrument).

**Study notes**
- **Nose-high recovery (IFH Ch 5):** add **power**, **reduce pitch** (forward pressure), **level the wings** — then return to level flight. Airspeed decreasing = nose-high.
- **Nose-low recovery:** **reduce power**, **level the wings first**, then **raise the nose** gently — rolling before pulling avoids tightening a spiral and over-stressing the airframe. Airspeed increasing = nose-low.
- Trust the instruments over the body (IFH Ch 1): recognizing that the vestibular system lies is the entire point of this task. The attitude indicator can itself be the failure — cross-check before recovering (K2/R4).
